Black-Litterman & Mean-CVaR — Guide
Where returns come from, how risk is measured, and how the frontier is traced

This optimiser answers two questions separately. What return do we expect? is handled by the Black-Litterman model, which blends the market's own implied views with analyst price targets. How much can we lose? is handled by Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR), the average loss in the worst tail of days. The efficient frontier then trades expected return against CVaR — not against variance.

Return engine — Black-Litterman
Using historical average returns as the forecast is famously fragile: the optimiser amplifies estimation noise into extreme corner portfolios. Black-Litterman (1990) instead starts from the returns the market already implies and nudges them with your views. The equilibrium prior is recovered by reverse optimisation from the market-cap weights wmkt:
$$\Pi = \delta\,\Sigma\,w_{\mathrm{mkt}}, \qquad \delta = \frac{\text{risk premium}}{w_{\mathrm{mkt}}^\top \Sigma\, w_{\mathrm{mkt}}}$$
Π is the excess return that would make today's market-cap weights optimal for a representative investor with risk-aversion δ. Market caps are FX-normalised to a common currency first, so a ¥-quoted mega-cap doesn't swamp the prior on units alone.
Views come from 12-month analyst price targets — one absolute view per covered asset, qi = targeti/pricei − 1 − rf (excess). Each view's uncertainty Ωii tightens with more analysts and lower target dispersion. The posterior combines prior and views:
$$\mu_{\mathrm{BL}} = \Pi + \tau\Sigma P^\top\!\left(P\,\tau\Sigma\,P^\top + \Omega\right)^{-1}\!\left(Q - P\Pi\right)$$
The Analyst trust dial is a single global scalar in [0, 1] that scales Ω. At 0% the views vanish (Ω → ∞) and the posterior collapses to the pure market prior; at 100% (Ω → 0) it snaps to the analyst targets. Assets with no target simply keep the prior — no view is invented for them.
Risk engine — CVaR on daily scenarios
Variance penalises upside and downside alike. CVaRα (expected shortfall) instead measures only the left tail: the average loss on the worst (1−α) fraction of days. Scenarios are the raw daily FX-adjusted returns over the lookback (~750 for 3Y), so the tail is empirical — no normality assumption. The X-axis is annualised (×√252) so it is comparable to the return axis.
For a target expected return, the minimum-CVaR portfolio is the Rockafellar-Uryasev (2000) linear program, which linearises the tail average exactly:
$$\min_{w,\zeta,u}\ \zeta + \frac{1}{(1-\alpha)T}\sum_{t} u_t \quad \text{s.t.}\quad u_t \ge -R_t^\top w - \zeta,\ \ u_t \ge 0,\ \ \mu_{\mathrm{BL}}^\top w \ge R^\star$$
$$w \in [w_{\min}, w_{\max}], \qquad w^\top\mathbf{1} = 1\ \ (\text{or } \le 1 \text{ with cash})$$
At the optimum ζ⋆ = VaRα and the objective equals CVaRα. Sweeping the return floor R⋆ from the global min-CVaR portfolio up to the max-return portfolio traces the whole return-vs-CVaR frontier — the curve the risk slider rides.
The LP is solved by a bespoke numba primal-dual interior-point method that eliminates the T scenario variables analytically each Newton step, collapsing the linear system to an (N+1)×(N+1) solve — the whole frontier in well under 100 ms, with no scipy on the hot path. A reference HiGHS solver certifies it to 1e-6 in the test-suite.
What's shown
  • Expected return (BL) — the posterior return of the selected portfolio, annualised.
  • CVaR95 — the risk axis: annualised expected shortfall at the chosen confidence.
  • CDaR / Max drawdown — the deepest and average-worst peak-to-trough declines of the selected weights over the lookback path. Shown, not optimised.
  • Vol (reference) — annualised standard deviation, for context only.
  • Cloud — random long-only portfolios rebased into (CVaR, return) space, so you can see the frontier really is the upper edge.
Limitations
  • Analyst targets are noisy. The trust dial exists precisely so you can dial their influence down; at 0% you get the pure market prior.
  • Tail estimation needs data. A 99% CVaR on one year of daily data rests on only ~2–3 tail days; longer lookbacks give steadier tails.
  • Covariance still matters. It drives the BL prior even though risk is CVaR-based — Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage stabilises it.
  • Long-only. No shorting or leverage; the fully-invested toggle only relaxes to holding cash.
  • Backward-looking. Both the scenarios and the covariance reflect the chosen lookback; the frontier is not a forecast.

Portfolio _App — Guide
Feature library, workflow, and column formulas

Data is fetched live from Yahoo Finance via yfinance. Each row is one security. Click any row for a detail view. Use ⇅ Sort to reorder by any visible metric. Colors are heatmaps, and most controls in the top bar and column bar explain themselves on hover.

Workflow
Open Portfolio to manage saved tabs and constituents. Build or update the table, then move across analytics, FX denomination, column views, the analyst dashboard, and the row detail modal without losing the underlying portfolio state.
  • Use saved tabs as named portfolio workspaces with cached rows, stale markers, last-view restore, and inline rename.
  • Build or refresh once, then inspect the same holdings through the table, analytics, sentiment, FX, and detail layers.
  • Switch weighting modes and time windows to see how the same portfolio behaves under different assumptions.
  • Export the whole saved state to Excel when you want a portable research artifact.
Why it matters:This app is designed to move from idea capture to ranking, risk review, analyst context, and export without making you rebuild the portfolio in separate tools.
Portfolio Workspace
Saved portfolio tabs hold constituents, cached rows, and analytics context. Tabs can be renamed inline and carry stale state when the saved view no longer matches live data.
  • Saved tabs and an ad hoc current tab.
  • Inline rename, delete, and restore-last-view behavior.
  • Cached row payloads so previously built views reopen instantly.
Useful because:it turns the dashboard into a persistent research workspace instead of a one-shot ticker paste box.
Build & Refresh
Dashboard builds stream row-by-row, so price, momentum, valuation, and technical fields appear progressively instead of waiting for the full portfolio to finish.
  • Streaming quote build and update path.
  • Per-row error states when Yahoo has no usable data.
  • Smart primary action that flips between Build Dashboard and Update Portfolio.
Useful because:you get fast feedback on large portfolios and can spot broken symbols without blocking the whole run.
Portfolio Analytics
The analytics panel turns the holdings table into a portfolio object with benchmark-relative risk and return math.
  • Equal-weight, cap-weighted, and custom-weight modes.
  • 3M, 6M, YTD, 1Y, 3Y, 5Y, and Max windows.
  • SPY, NASDAQ, sector-mix, and drawdown overlays.
  • Risk & Return, weighted valuation, exposure, concentration, and contribution blocks.
Useful because:you can judge whether an idea is attractive as a portfolio, not just as a collection of individually interesting names.
Analyst Sentiment
A separate dashboard aggregates sell-side coverage across the active holdings.
  • Weighted consensus rating on the 1 to 5 Yahoo scale.
  • Coverage-weighted target upside.
  • Recommendation distribution and per-holding consensus table.
  • Coverage gaps so you can see what part of the book analysts do not cover.
Useful because:it separates market-implied valuation from street expectations and shows where your book is consensus or contrarian.
FX & Denomination
The display currency selector converts prices, portfolio values, and analytics into a consistent denomination while keeping the original quote context available.
  • Spot FX conversion across the supported major currencies.
  • Hoverable 1Y synthetic currency basket index for context.
  • FX-adjusted portfolio analytics in the chosen display currency.
Useful because:it keeps return comparisons honest when the portfolio mixes USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF, or CAD exposures.
Column Views & Sorting
The table is no longer a single fixed layout. You can switch the lens instead of forcing every idea through one preset.
  • Built-in presets for Default, Fundamentals, and Momentum.
  • Custom column views saved separately from portfolios.
  • Drag-to-reorder headers and a dirty-state indicator for modified presets.
  • Fit to screen compaction and a Sort menu keyed to the active visible columns.
Useful because:screen real estate becomes a deliberate ranking tool, so you can pivot from momentum review to quality review without rebuilding data.
Row Detail Modal
Clicking a row opens the heavy research view for one security.
  • 1M to Max chart windows with benchmark and volume overlays.
  • Snapshot, valuation, profitability, fundamentals, dividend, profile, and news sections.
  • Analyst targets, consensus trend, and benchmark-relative performance.
Useful because:the table stays fast, while the modal becomes the deep-dive layer for understanding what is driving a single name.
Excel Export
Export produces one workbook across every saved portfolio, not just the active tab.
  • Overview sheet plus one sheet per saved portfolio.
  • Holdings data, analyst fields, exposure, concentration, and portfolio analytics blocks.
  • Designed as a portable input artifact for spreadsheet work or AI-assisted review.
Useful because:it gives you a complete offline snapshot of what the app can already see, without re-querying everything manually.
Column Families Beyond The Default View
The app exposes more than the original default table. Optional columns already available today include:
  • Momentum and technical fields: % 1W, % 1M, % 3M, % 6M, RSI 14, MACD %, Bollinger %B, 20MA, 50MA, and 200MA.
  • Fundamental and balance-sheet fields: Sector, Industry, Beta, Forward P/E, PEG, EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, Operating Margin, D/E, Current Ratio, and Dividend Yield.
  • Range and analyst fields: 52W High, 52W Low, Analyst Rating, and Target Δ.
Useful because:you can choose whether the table is a momentum board, a fundamentals screen, a risk dashboard, or an analyst-consensus monitor.
Core Column Reference
The cards below cover the columns in the default view plus several optional metrics that matter when you switch presets.
Ticker
Exchange symbol used to identify the security (e.g. AAPL, NVDA, XLK). Company names are resolved automatically.
Company
Full legal name of the company or fund as reported by Yahoo Finance.
Price
Last available closing price, converted to the selected display currency (see FX selector in the top bar).
Market Cap
Total market value of all outstanding shares.
$$\text{Market Cap} = P \times \text{Shares Outstanding}$$
P/S — Price-to-Sales
How much investors pay per dollar of trailing-12-month revenue. Useful for companies with zero or negative earnings. Heat anchors at P/S = 10 (expensive); n/a is also flagged.
$$P/S = \dfrac{\text{Market Cap}}{\text{TTM Revenue}}$$
P/E — Price-to-Earnings
Most widely-used valuation multiple. Heat anchors at P/E = 40; values above 50 imply heavy growth expectations being priced in.
$$P/E = \dfrac{P}{\text{EPS}_{\text{TTM}}}$$
% YTD
Return from the first trading day of the current calendar year to today. Green = positive, red = negative, white = flat.
$$\text{YTD} = \left(\dfrac{P_1}{P_0} - 1\right) \times 100$$ P₀ = first close of the calendar year
Chart 1Y
Sparkline of the last 252 trading days' closing prices. Line is green if the 1-year return is positive, red otherwise. Click the row for a full-size chart.
% 1Y
Total price return over the last 365 calendar days. Same diverging color scale as % YTD.
$$\text{1Y} = \left(\dfrac{P_1}{P_0} - 1\right) \times 100$$ P₀ = close 365 calendar days ago
Δ Highs — Distance from 2Y High
How far the current price sits below the highest close in the table's fast-path history window (currently 2 years). 0% means the stock is at that 2Y high. The detail modal still shows a full-history ATH separately.
$$\Delta\text{2YH} = \left(\dfrac{P}{P_{\text{2Y High}}} - 1\right) \times 100$$
RS Rank 1M — Relative Strength
Histogram of 12 monthly bars. Each bar's height shows where that month's closing price ranked within the stock's own trailing 12-month price range. Taller, brighter green = stronger relative position.
$$\text{RS} = \dfrac{P_{m} - \text{Low}_{12m}}{\text{High}_{12m} - \text{Low}_{12m}} \;\in\; [0,\,1]$$
20 / 50 / 200 SMA
Simple Moving Average flags. ▲ = price is above the SMA (bullish momentum). ▼ = price is below (bearish). The three periods correspond to roughly 1 month, 1 quarter, and 1 year of trading days.
$$\text{SMA}_n = \dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} P_i$$
Forward P/E
Price divided by consensus forward earnings. It tells you what multiple the market is paying on the next year rather than the trailing year.
$$\text{Forward P/E} = \dfrac{P}{\text{EPS}_{\text{NTM}}}$$
EV/EBITDA
Enterprise-value multiple that normalises for capital structure. Useful when debt loads differ materially across peers.
$$\text{EV/EBITDA} = \dfrac{\text{Enterprise Value}}{\text{EBITDA}}$$
Analyst Rating
Mean sell-side recommendation on Yahoo's 1 to 5 scale, where 1 is Strong Buy and 5 is Sell.
$$R = \dfrac{\sum_{j=1}^{N} r_j}{N},\quad 1=\text{Strong Buy},\; 5=\text{Sell}$$
Target Δ
Distance from the current price to the mean analyst target price. Positive values imply upside to consensus fair value.
$$\Delta_{target} = \left(\dfrac{TP_{mean}}{P} - 1\right) \times 100$$
